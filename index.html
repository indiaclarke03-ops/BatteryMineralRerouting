Is Chinese battery graphite reaching US battery plants through factories in other countries?

Chinese companies are building battery-material factories in Indonesia, Morocco, Oman and elsewhere. Their output can reach the US looking "non-Chinese". We followed the paper trail for five of these plants through trade and company records, and we show every link we found and how sure we are of each one.

The story in 60 seconds

Why this matters

1 · The material

Every EV battery needs graphite

A lithium-ion battery has two electrodes. The anode side is almost always graphite, mined (natural) or made from petroleum coke (artificial). Graphite is roughly a fifth of a battery cell's weight.

2 · The supplier

China makes almost all of it

China produces over 90% of the world's battery anode material. US cell plants (Ultium, SK, Tesla and others) still need it. So Chinese makers like BTR and Zhongke are opening factories in other countries.

3 · The rule

The US now asks who owns the factory

Since July 2025, US battery makers can lose clean-energy tax credits if too much of a part's cost comes from a "prohibited foreign entity", in practice often a Chinese-owned company. Anti-dumping duties were voted down in March 2026, so ownership is now the main lever.

What the five cases tell us

Finding 1

Trade shows the path; registries show the owner, but rarely both

For BTR Indonesia, Sayari shows the shipments but not the owner. For BTR Morocco and Putailai Malaysia, it shows the owner but no shipments yet. A full picture needs both at once.

Finding 2

Same product, same buyers, different owners

BTR Indonesia (Chinese-owned) and POSCO Future M (Korean-owned) both ship artificial graphite to GM, SK Battery America and Tesla. Shipping records alone can't tell them apart. Ownership data can.

Finding 3

The next wave isn't shipping yet

Chinese-owned anode plants in Morocco, Oman and Malaysia are all under construction, with no graphite trade in Sayari. Today is the time to record their ownership, before goods start to move.

Finding 4

Country totals mislead

3,088 "graphite" rows from Morocco were mostly mislabelled fertilizer. 917 rows from Oman were mostly Indian cargo passing through. You have to read the records, not just the counts.

    Verdict for this case: what Sayari could and couldn't see

    Why now

    The rules changed. Ownership is now the pressure point

    Duties on Chinese anode material were voted down in March 2026. The tax-credit test on who owns suppliers stays. Every date below was checked against a source.

    Plain English

    Key terms

    Anode
    The negative side of a battery. In lithium-ion batteries it's almost always graphite.
    Natural vs. artificial graphite
    Natural graphite is mined (HS 2504). Artificial graphite is made by baking petroleum coke (HS 3801). Battery anodes use both.
    HS code
    The product code on every customs record. HS 3801.10 = artificial graphite; 2504 = natural graphite; 2713 = petroleum coke.
    Prohibited foreign entity (PFE)
    A company owned or controlled by a "foreign entity of concern" such as China. US battery makers lose tax credits if too much of a component's cost comes from PFEs.
    Anti-dumping / countervailing duties
    Extra tariffs on goods sold below fair value or subsidised. Commerce proposed them on Chinese anode material; the USITC voted them down.
    Third-country plant
    A factory outside China, often Chinese-owned, that processes Chinese inputs before export.
    Sayari
    A database linking company registries, ownership and customs records from 250+ jurisdictions. We queried it through its MCP server.
    Entity resolution
    Deciding whether several database records are really one company. Customs records spell names differently, so one factory can appear many times.
