The story in 60 seconds

Why this matters

1 · The material

Every EV battery needs graphite

A lithium-ion battery has two electrodes. The anode side is almost always graphite, mined (natural) or made from petroleum coke (artificial). Graphite is roughly a fifth of a battery cell's weight.

2 · The supplier

China makes almost all of it

China produces over 90% of the world's battery anode material. US cell plants (Ultium, SK, Tesla and others) still need it. So Chinese makers like BTR and Zhongke are opening factories in other countries.

3 · The rule

The US now asks who owns the factory

Since July 2025, US battery makers can lose clean-energy tax credits if too much of a part's cost comes from a "prohibited foreign entity", in practice often a Chinese-owned company. Anti-dumping duties were voted down in March 2026, so ownership is now the main lever.

What the cases tell us

  1. 01

    Shipping records show the route; company registries show the owner, but rarely both

    For BTR Indonesia, Sayari shows the shipments but not the owner. For BTR Morocco and Putailai Malaysia, it shows the owner before the plant exports anything. For Gotion in the US, the owner is visible only from the Chinese side.

    Cases 1 2 5 7

  2. 02

    Same product, same buyers, different owners

    BTR Indonesia (Chinese-owned) and POSCO Future M (Korean-owned) both ship artificial graphite to GM, SK Battery America and Tesla, and BTR now also supplies Ford. Shipping records alone can't tell them apart; ownership data can.

    Cases 1 4 8 9

  3. 03

    Graphite can arrive inside finished battery cells

    EVE's and Linergy's Malaysian plants take in Chinese anode graphite from BTR, Zhongke and Shanshan, and ship finished cells to Briggs & Stratton, Milwaukee Tool, Eaton and others. The graphite never appears in US customs data as graphite. The same is true inside the EU, where trade between member states leaves no customs record.

    Cases 6 10 11

  4. 04

    The next wave isn't shipping yet

    Chinese-owned anode plants in Morocco, Oman and Malaysia are still being built or ramping up, and none exports yet. BTR's Moroccan plant took its first graphite, from BTR's own Indonesian plant, in Aug 2026. Their ownership is already on record, so buyers can know who owns them before the first export.

    Cases 2 3 5 · Watch list

  5. 05

    Country of origin doesn't tell you who owns the plant

    3,088 "graphite" rows from Morocco were mostly mislabelled fertilizer, and most of the 917 rows from Oman were Indian cargo relayed through Omani ports. One Mozambique mine supplies both a US plant and Chinese-owned plants.

    Cases 2 3 8

  6. 06

    Owners change faster than registries do

    Japan's TDK bought the Malaysian cell maker Linergy Power from CATL's Ampace in June 2026, but in October Sayari still showed the old Chinese owners. BTR's 60% of its Indonesian plant is in BTR's own filings, not the registry. Combine registry data with company filings, and re-check.

Plain English

Key terms

Show the eight terms used on this site
Anode
The negative side of a battery. In lithium-ion batteries it's almost always graphite.
Natural vs. artificial graphite
Natural graphite is mined (HS 2504). Artificial graphite is made by baking petroleum coke (HS 3801). Battery anodes use both.
HS code
The product code on every customs record. HS 3801.10 = artificial graphite; 2504 = natural graphite; 2713 = petroleum coke.
Prohibited foreign entity (PFE)
A company owned or controlled by a "foreign entity of concern" such as China. US battery makers lose tax credits if too much of a component's cost comes from PFEs.
Anti-dumping / countervailing duties
Extra tariffs on goods sold below fair value or subsidised. Commerce proposed them on Chinese anode material; the USITC voted them down.
Third-country plant
A factory outside China, often Chinese-owned, that processes Chinese inputs before export.
Sayari
A database linking company registries, ownership and customs records from 250+ jurisdictions. We queried it through its MCP server.
Entity resolution
Deciding whether several database records are really one company. Customs records spell names differently, so one factory can appear many times.
